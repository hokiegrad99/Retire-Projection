MASTER PROJECT PROMPT: LOCAL-FIRST RETIREMENT PLANNING PLATFORM
1. YOUR ROLE AND MISSION

Act as my principal software architect, senior full-stack developer, financial modeling engineer, database architect, UI/UX designer, security engineer, QA engineer, and technical project manager.

Build a comprehensive, locally hosted personal retirement and financial planning application that combines the breadth of capabilities found in commercial retirement planning platforms such as ProjectionLab and Boldin.

The application must be original software. Use those products only as references for publicly described features, workflows, and general usability concepts. Do not copy proprietary source code, protected assets, branding, text, or exact interface designs.

The objective is to build a practical, reliable, extensible application that I own and control, without a required subscription to a commercial retirement planning platform.

Do not merely produce a plan, mockup, clickable prototype, or collection of disconnected screens. Build a working application with persistent data, a functional financial calculation engine, interactive scenarios, automated tests, documentation, and a repeatable deployment process.

Work incrementally over multiple AI sessions if necessary. Project continuity and recovery from lost conversation history are mandatory requirements.

2. PRIMARY OBJECTIVES

The application must:

Run locally on my computer or a home server, preferably using Docker Compose.
Work without requiring a public cloud service or an internet connection for ordinary planning tasks.
Store financial information locally in a database that I control.
Support detailed retirement projections from the current year through a configurable longevity age.
Model individuals, couples, dependents, assets, liabilities, income, expenses, investments, healthcare, taxes, Social Security, and estate objectives.
Support saved baseline plans, independent what-if scenarios, scenario comparisons, and strategy optimization.
Include Monte Carlo simulations and deterministic projections with transparent assumptions.
Provide clear dashboards, charts, reports, data import, data export, and backups.
Include advanced features comparable in purpose to premium retirement planning tools, prioritizing financial accuracy over superficial feature count.
Preserve project state, implementation status, architectural decisions, unresolved issues, and the next actionable development task in files stored in the project repository.
Resume safely after a crash, lost AI conversation, context-window exhaustion, or a break of several days.
Remain usable without AI features. Any optional AI assistant must be separable from the core financial calculation engine.

7. DETERMINISTIC FINANCIAL PROJECTION ENGINE

This is the central component of the application.

Implement a transparent, deterministic projection engine that calculates results year by year, with the option for monthly calculations where necessary for timing-sensitive events.

The engine must model:

Current balances and initial net worth.
Employment income and retirement transitions.
Savings and employer contributions.
Investment growth and fees.
Contributions, withdrawals, and account transfers.
Inflation-adjusted income and expenses.
Taxes and tax withholding assumptions.
Social Security and pension income.
Debt amortization.
Healthcare and Medicare costs.
Required minimum distributions.
Roth conversions.
Real estate appreciation, mortgage amortization, and sale events.
Major one-time cash flows.
Asset depletion and shortfall events.
Estate value at death or the selected longevity horizon.
Calculation requirements
Use a consistent annual calculation sequence and document it.
Define the order in which income, investment returns, contributions, taxes, withdrawals, and expenses occur.
Avoid circular calculations and double counting.
Handle partial retirement years and changes in household status.
Define how negative cash flow is funded.
Distinguish taxable income from cash income and account withdrawals.
Keep account-specific tax treatment explicit.
Track balances by account and tax category.
Record unmet spending needs and insolvency years.
Report assumptions and calculation warnings alongside results.

Use decimal arithmetic for currency calculations where appropriate. Document rounding rules. Use numerically appropriate methods for probabilities and statistical simulations.

Do not silently treat an unfunded expense as paid or allow a depleted account to acquire a negative balance without an explicit borrowing rule.

The calculation engine must return structured, machine-readable outputs, including annual income, expenses, taxes, contributions, withdrawals, account balances, net worth, estate value, and shortfall information.

Maximizing projected after-tax estate value.

Social Security optimizer

Retirement date optimizer

Search for the earliest feasible retirement date under configurable spending, longevity, and risk criteria.

Spending optimizer

Estimate sustainable spending levels under explicit deterministic or probabilistic constraints.

Withdrawal and asset-location optimization

Optimization standards
Specify the objective function and constraints.
Identify the metric being minimized or maximized.
Display assumptions, trade-offs, and limitations.
Compare the selected result with the baseline.
Preserve the original plan.
Validate optimized results with independent projection runs.
Avoid claiming a global optimum unless the algorithm establishes one.
Use sensitivity analysis where practical.

20. TESTING AND FINANCIAL CORRECTNESS

Build tests alongside each module, not at the end.

Unit tests

Test individual financial functions, validation rules, tax calculations, account rules, inflation adjustments, amortization, and withdrawal calculations.

Integration tests

Test the interaction between accounts, income, expenses, taxes, Social Security, withdrawals, and the projection engine.

Regression tests

Maintain version-controlled reference scenarios with expected outputs and explicit tolerances. When a calculation changes, explain and review the expected differences.

Property and invariant tests

Test properties such as:

A zero-return, zero-contribution account remains unchanged unless a transaction occurs.
Contributions and withdrawals reconcile to ending balances.
Assets minus liabilities equal net worth.
A scenario does not modify the baseline.
Identical inputs and random seeds reproduce simulation results.
Depleted accounts do not silently fund additional spending.
Taxes are not double-counted.
Cash flows reconcile within documented rounding tolerances.
Reference validation

Use authoritative, applicable tax and benefit references. Record the source, effective tax year, reference case, expected result, and actual result.

Do not mark a tax or benefits module fully validated solely because it produces plausible-looking numbers.
